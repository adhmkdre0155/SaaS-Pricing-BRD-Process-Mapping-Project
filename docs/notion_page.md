### The problem
A SaaS company wants to launch a new self-serve tiered pricing plan and needs a BA to define requirements, map the new billing process, and flag risks — before development starts.

### Why this project has no dataset
On purpose. A Data Analyst portfolio proves you can find a story in data. This project proves something a DA portfolio can't: operating from stakeholder conversations and ambiguity alone, before there's any data to analyze — which is exactly what differentiates a BA portfolio.

### What I did
1. **Wrote a BRD** — stakeholders, current-state pain points, the proposed 3-tier pricing structure, explicit scope boundaries, and success metrics.
2. **Wrote 7 user stories** in standard format, each with concrete, testable acceptance criteria — engineering-ready, not vague.
3. **Mapped current vs. future process** as swimlane diagrams, showing exactly where the current bottleneck sits and how the new self-serve flow removes it.
4. **Built a RACI matrix** across 10 rollout tasks and 6 roles (Product, Engineering, Finance, Support, Sales, BA).
5. **Wrote a risk log** — 7 identified risks (proration edge cases, currency handling, migration churn risk, and more) with likelihood, impact, mitigation, and owner.

### 🔑 Key insight
> The highest-leverage decision in this project wasn't a number — it was resolving one ambiguity before any design work started: auto-migrate existing customers to the new tiers, or give them an explicit choice with advance notice? I committed to the latter (30-day notice + customer choice) directly because Sales flagged renewal churn as their top concern — and that single resolved ambiguity shapes 3 of the 7 user stories and 2 of the 7 logged risks.

### Recommendation
Proceed to engineering discovery using the BRD and user stories as baseline scope, resolving the two flagged dependencies (payment provider proration API, Finance system feed compatibility) as the first technical-discovery questions.

### Business impact
Signals BA maturity beyond spreadsheets: requirements-gathering, cross-functional coordination, and artifacts a real engineering team could start building from directly.
